Understanding Aari Embroidery

Understanding Aari Embroidery

Aari is a traditional Indian embroidery technique done with a hooked needle called an Aari needle. It's used to pull thread loops through fabric from below, creating fine continuous chain stitch patterns. It's one of the most intricate forms of hand embroidery and is especially popular for bridal and occasion wear in South India.
An Aari needle is a specialised hooked embroidery needle used to create chain stitches. Its hooked tip helps the artisan pull thread through the fabric repeatedly, making it suitable for detailed embroidery patterns.
Aari describes the hooked-needle chain-stitch technique. Maggam commonly refers to embroidery worked on a frame and is sometimes used interchangeably with Aari in local usage. Zardosi describes metallic-thread and embellishment styles that may be worked using Aari techniques. Names vary by region and studio, so ask about the specific materials and method used for a design.
Silk, raw silk, velvet, georgette, and net are the most popular. Cotton and linen can also be embroidered but require additional backing. Stretch fabrics like lycra are not suitable as the embroidery restricts movement. Share your fabric type when you enquire and we'll advise accordingly.
Always dry clean Aari embroidered pieces. Avoid machine washing as agitation can loosen threads and dislodge stones. Store flat or rolled - do not fold at the embroidered section. Keep away from moisture and direct sunlight. A light pressing with a cloth on the reverse side is safe if needed.

Care & Maintenance

Caring for Aari Embroidery

Avoid machine washing because agitation can loosen threads and damage embellishments. Dry cleaning is generally recommended for heavily embellished garments; follow care instructions for the specific fabric.
Professional dry cleaning is generally recommended, particularly for garments with beads, stones or metallic embellishments. Inform the cleaner about the embroidery.
Store it in a clean, dry place away from sunlight and moisture. Use a soft protective cover and avoid placing heavy objects on embroidered areas.
Avoid pulling threads, rough surfaces and aggressive washing. Store the blouse carefully and handle it gently when wearing or transporting it.
Lifespan depends on materials, workmanship, use and maintenance. Proper cleaning, careful handling and suitable storage can help preserve the embroidery for many years.
