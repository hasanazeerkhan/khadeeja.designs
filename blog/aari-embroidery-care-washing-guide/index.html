Aari embroidery care and washing guide

Hand embroidery, delicate fabric and attached embellishments need thoughtful care. Use this guide to store and clean an Aari blouse while avoiding common sources of damage.

Aari work blouses can include thread, beads, stones, sequins, metallic details and delicate backing. Their care needs vary with the fabric and materials, so check the garment label and ask the embroiderer or a trusted specialist cleaner before washing. When in doubt, avoid experimenting on the full garment.

Important: This is general care guidance, not a substitute for the care label or advice from a cleaner familiar with embellished garments. Some fabrics, dyes, adhesives and embellishments cannot tolerate water or solvent cleaning.

After wearing: air and store carefully

Let the blouse air in a shaded, ventilated place before putting it away. Do not leave it damp with sweat or perfume, and keep it away from direct sun, heaters and moisture. Store it clean and completely dry in a breathable garment bag or clean cotton cover. Avoid crushing raised embroidery under heavy garments.

For a structured blouse, support the shape with acid-free tissue or a soft cloth rather than pressing tissue directly against delicate stones. Fold as little as possible; if folding is necessary, keep the folds away from heavily embroidered sections.

Spot cleaning and stain response

  • Act promptly by gently blotting a fresh spill with a clean, dry, lint-free cloth. Do not rub or scrape the embroidery.
  • Keep stain removers, bleach, alcohol, perfume and household detergents away from the work unless a professional confirms they are safe.
  • Do not pull at a loose thread, bead or stone. Secure the garment and ask an experienced tailor or embroiderer to assess it.
  • If the mark remains, take the blouse to a cleaner and explain the fabric and embellishments before treatment.

Can you wash an Aari work blouse?

There is no universal washing method for every Aari blouse. Water can affect colour, shrinkage, backing, metallic details, adhesives and some stones. Machine washing, wringing, soaking and aggressive hand washing can snag threads or loosen embellishments. Read the label first. If it says dry clean only or you are unsure about the materials use a professional cleaner experienced with embroidered garments.

If a care professional confirms that gentle hand washing is suitable for the exact garment, follow their instructions precisely: use cool water and a mild product only if approved, do not scrub embroidered areas, do not twist or wring, and rinse without prolonged soaking. Test for colour transfer only when the cleaner or garment maker advises a safe, inconspicuous test. Lay flat to dry away from heat and sunlight; never tumble dry.

Pressing and packing for travel

Do not place a hot iron directly on raised embroidery, stones or metallic work. Use the garment label and a professional's guidance; a pressing cloth and low heat may still be unsuitable for some materials. For travel, place clean tissue or soft cotton between embroidered sections and pack the blouse so the work is not sharply creased or compressed.
